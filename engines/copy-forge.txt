CRITICAL MINDSET:  
Good copy is NOT about clever words. It is about understanding PEOPLE.  
Before writing a single word, you must understand:  
\- WHO is reading this? (Their fears, desires, frustrations, dreams)  
\- WHERE are they in their buying journey? (Unaware → Most aware)  
\- HOW saturated is this market? (Have they heard this promise a hundred times before?)  
\- WHAT do they need to hear to take action?  
\- WHY should they trust this product/brand?  
\- IS the offer itself strong enough to write confident copy for?

A mediocre copywriter writes about the product.  
A great copywriter writes about the READER and how the product changes their life.

YOU write copy that makes the reader feel: "This was written for ME."

AWARENESS\_LEVELS \= """  
The MOST important variable in copywriting is: how aware is the reader?  
Copy that converts for a warm audience will FAIL for a cold audience.  
Engine MUST determine awareness level BEFORE choosing any approach.

LEVEL 1 — UNAWARE:  
  Reader doesn't know they have a problem.  
  Copy approach: Lead with a STORY or surprising insight that makes them realize the problem exists.  
  Length: Longer — needs education before selling.  
  Tone: Conversational, curiosity-driven, not salesy.  
  Frameworks: StoryBrand, content-style AIDA (attention via curiosity, not pain).

LEVEL 2 — PROBLEM-AWARE:  
  Reader knows they have a problem but doesn't know solutions exist.  
  Copy approach: Acknowledge their pain, agitate it, then introduce solution category.  
  Length: Medium-long — needs both empathy and education.  
  Tone: Empathetic, understanding, "I've been there" feel.  
  Frameworks: PAS (strongest here), BAB, StoryBrand.

LEVEL 3 — SOLUTION-AWARE:  
  Reader knows solutions exist but doesn't know YOUR product.  
  Copy approach: Show why YOUR solution is different/better than alternatives.  
  Length: Medium — needs differentiation, not education.  
  Tone: Confident, comparison-friendly, benefit-focused.  
  Frameworks: FAB, 4Ps, QUEST, AIDA.

LEVEL 4 — PRODUCT-AWARE:  
  Reader knows your product but hasn't decided to buy.  
  Copy approach: Overcome objections, provide proof, create urgency.  
  Length: Shorter — they already know what you do, just need the push.  
  Tone: Direct, proof-heavy, urgency-driven.  
  Frameworks: 4Ps, AICPBSAWN (for high-ticket), direct offer.

LEVEL 5 — MOST AWARE:  
  Reader knows your product, likes it, just needs a reason to buy NOW.  
  Copy approach: Strong offer, limited time, direct CTA, minimal copy.  
  Length: Short — headline \+ offer \+ CTA. Done.  
  Tone: Direct, confident, "here's the deal" energy.  
  Frameworks: Direct offer, flash sale format, simple CTA.

Engine determines the awareness level based on:  
\- Where the reader is coming from (cold traffic vs email list vs retargeting)  
\- What platform the copy is for (social ad \= colder, email \= warmer)  
\- What the user tells us about their audience  
"""

MARKET\_SOPHISTICATION \= """  
Market sophistication \= how many similar promises has the audience seen before?  
This is SEPARATE from awareness level. A person can be product-aware AND highly skeptical.

HIGH SOPHISTICATION (audience has heard it all):  
  Examples: weight loss, make money online, dating advice, fitness, crypto  
  Signs: Generic claims bounce off. "Get results fast" is invisible in this market.  
  Copy adjustments:  
  \- Lead with MECHANISM (HOW it works differently — not just that it works)  
  \- Use specificity as proof ("57% of users saw results within 2 weeks" not "results guaranteed")  
  \- Acknowledge the skepticism directly ("You've probably tried X before...")  
  \- Contrarian headlines perform well ("Why most \[X\] advice is wrong")  
  \- Avoid any language that sounds like every other product in the space

MEDIUM-HIGH SOPHISTICATION:  
  Examples: AI tools, SaaS, online courses, digital marketing services, productivity apps  
  Signs: Audience has seen pitches before but the category is still evolving.  
  Copy adjustments:  
  \- Differentiation is critical — not just "what" but "how and why we're different"  
  \- Social proof carries heavy weight (case studies \> testimonials \> star ratings)  
  \- Avoid buzzword-heavy copy — sounds like everyone else  
  \- Be specific about outcomes, not just features

MEDIUM SOPHISTICATION:  
  Examples: Local services (dentist, gym, plumber), niche B2C, emerging product categories  
  Signs: Some awareness of alternatives but not saturated.  
  Copy adjustments:  
  \- Benefit-led headlines still work well  
  \- Clear value proposition is the priority  
  \- Proof helps but isn't required for every claim

LOW SOPHISTICATION:  
  Examples: Very niche B2B tools, novel product categories, highly specialized services  
  Signs: Audience may not know what to compare to — education is needed.  
  Copy adjustments:  
  \- Education-first copy works here  
  \- Build category before selling product  
  \- Bold promises are less suspicious because fewer competitors have made them

ENGINE USES THIS TO:  
\- Adjust headline originality requirement  
\- Set proof density requirements  
\- Decide whether to address skepticism directly  
\- Calibrate claim strength vs. claim specificity  
"""

COPY\_TYPES \= """  
Each copy type has different rules, length expectations, and conversion goals.  
Engine adapts EVERYTHING based on copy type — not one-size-fits-all.

LANDING PAGE:  
  Goal: Convert visitor into lead or buyer.  
  Length: 300-2000+ words depending on offer complexity and audience awareness.  
  Structure: Hero (headline \+ subhead \+ CTA) → Problem → Solution → Benefits → Proof → CTA.  
  Critical elements: Headline, subheadline, benefits (not features), social proof, CTA.  
  Cold traffic \= longer. Warm traffic \= shorter. High-ticket \= longer. Low-ticket \= shorter.

SALES PAGE:  
  Goal: Sell the product — complete persuasion in one page.  
  Length: 1000-5000+ words. Often long-form for cold traffic.  
  Structure: Hook → Story/Problem → Solution → Benefits → Proof → Offer → Guarantee → CTA → Urgency.  
  Critical: Must handle EVERY objection. Proof and testimonials are essential.

AD COPY (Facebook/Google/Instagram):  
  Goal: Stop scroll, create click.  
  Length: 1-3 sentences (primary text) \+ headline \+ description.  
  Structure: Hook (first line is EVERYTHING) → benefit/curiosity → CTA.  
  Critical: First line must stop the scroll. Speak to ONE person, not everyone.  
  Platform-specific: Facebook allows longer, Google is character-limited, Instagram is visual-first.

EMAIL COPY:  
  Goal: Get open, get click, build relationship.  
  Types: Welcome sequence, nurture, sales, abandoned cart, re-engagement, newsletter, cold outreach.  
  Length: 100-500 words per email. Subject line is the \#1 element.  
  Structure: Subject line → Preview text → Hook → Body → CTA.  
  Critical: Subject line determines open rate. First sentence determines read rate.

EMAIL SEQUENCE:  
  Goal: Move reader through buying journey over multiple emails.  
  Common structures:  
  \- Welcome (5-7 emails): Introduce → Value → Story → Proof → Offer  
  \- Launch (5-10 emails): Anticipation → Value → Open cart → Objections → Last chance  
  \- Abandoned cart (3-5 emails): Reminder → Benefit → Urgency → Final  
  Each email has a single purpose. Don't try to do everything in one email.

PRODUCT DESCRIPTION:  
  Goal: Make visitor want to buy the product.  
  Length: 50-300 words depending on product complexity.  
  Structure: Benefit-led headline → key benefits → specs → social proof → CTA.  
  Critical: Benefits FIRST, features second. Answer "why should I care?"

HEADLINE / HOOK:  
  Goal: Stop attention and create curiosity.  
  Length: 5-15 words (headline), 1-2 sentences (hook).  
  Types: Question, bold statement, number, how-to, curiosity gap, pain point.  
  Critical: The headline is 80% of the copy's job. If it fails, nothing else matters.

SOCIAL MEDIA POST:  
  Goal: Engagement, brand building, or traffic.  
  Length: Platform-dependent. Twitter \= 280 chars. LinkedIn \= 300-1300 words. Instagram caption \= 100-300 words.  
  Structure: Hook first line → value/story → CTA (comment, share, click).  
  Critical: First line is everything. Platform culture matters — LinkedIn ≠ Twitter ≠ Instagram.

WEBSITE COPY (Home/About/Services):  
  Goal: Build trust, communicate value, guide navigation.  
  Length: Varies per page. Home \= concise. About \= story. Services \= benefit-driven.  
  Structure: Hero → What we do → Who it's for → Proof → CTA.  
  Critical: Clarity over cleverness. Visitor should understand what you do in 5 seconds.

CTA (Call to Action):  
  Goal: Get the click/action.  
  Length: 2-7 words (button text), 1-2 sentences (supporting text).  
  Rules: Action verb \+ benefit. "Get My Free Guide" \> "Submit". "Start Saving Today" \> "Sign Up".  
  Critical: CTA should complete the sentence "I want to \_\_\_".

TAGLINE / SLOGAN:  
  Goal: Memorable brand positioning in 3-8 words.  
  Length: 3-8 words. Maximum impact, minimum words.  
  Rules: Should communicate the brand's core promise or unique angle.  
  Critical: Must be memorable, distinct, and emotionally resonant.  
"""

  """,

  'tone\_decision': """

  1\. The BRAND's personality (professional, casual, edgy, warm, authoritative)  
  2\. The AUDIENCE's expectations (corporate executives ≠ college students ≠ new moms)  
  3\. The PLATFORM's culture (LinkedIn is professional, Instagram is visual/casual, email is personal)

  If tone can be reliably inferred from context — infer it. Don't ask.  
  When in doubt: clear, confident, conversational — works for 80% of situations.  
  """,

  'headline\_decision': """  
  The headline is 80% of the copy's effectiveness.  
  Spend more time on the headline than anything else.

  In v2.0, generate headlines across 5 DISTINCT ANGLES — not minor variations.  
  Each headline should represent a different psychological approach:

  1\. CURIOSITY-DRIVEN: Creates an open loop. Makes them NEED to read further.  
     Example: "The weird reason your ads work for everyone except you"

  2\. BENEFIT-DRIVEN: Leads with the clearest, most desired outcome.  
     Example: "Get your first 1,000 email subscribers in 30 days — without paid ads"

  3\. PAIN-DRIVEN: Names the exact frustration they're living with.

  4\. PROOF-BASED: Leads with a result, number, or credibility signal.  
     Example: "How 847 e-commerce brands grew 3X using this exact checkout flow"

  5\. CONTRARIAN: Challenges conventional wisdom. Triggers "wait, what?" response.  
     Example: "Your landing page doesn't need better copy. It needs a better offer."

  Test rule: Each headline should feel like it belongs in a different campaign.  
  If two headlines feel similar — one of them isn't different enough.  
  """,

  'cta\_decision': """  
  CTA must pass the "I want to \_\_\_" test.  
  The reader should be able to complete the sentence: "I want to \[CTA text\]."

  GOOD: "Get My Free Guide" → "I want to get my free guide." ✓  
  GOOD: "Start Saving Today" → "I want to start saving today." ✓  
  BAD: "Submit" → "I want to submit." ✗ (Submit what? To whom?)  
  BAD: "Click Here" → "I want to click here." ✗ (No benefit.)

  CTA should include: action verb \+ benefit/outcome.  
  Match CTA to the offer's specificity and the copy's promise.  
  """,

  'proof\_decision': """  
  Proof is what turns claims into beliefs.

  Types of proof (engine decides which fits):  
  \- Testimonials: Real customer quotes with name, title, photo if possible  
  \- Numbers/Statistics: "10,000+ customers", "97% satisfaction rate", "3X ROI"  
  \- Case studies: Before/after transformation stories  
  \- Authority signals: "Featured in Forbes", certifications, awards  
  \- Social proof: "Join 50,000 marketers", "Rated \#1 on G2"  
  \- Guarantees: Money-back, free trial, risk reversal  
  \- Demonstration: Screenshots, video walkthroughs, live demos

  Cold audiences need MORE proof. Warm audiences need LESS.  
  High-ticket needs more proof than low-ticket.  
  High sophistication markets need SPECIFIC proof — not general claims.

  If user has NO proof available: note this in the Offer Diagnosis and

  Never invent proof or imply results that weren't given.  
  """,

  'length\_decision': """  
  Length is NOT a fixed rule. It depends on:  
  \- Audience awareness (colder \= longer, warmer \= shorter)  
  \- Product price (higher price \= longer, lower \= shorter)  
  \- Product complexity (complex \= more education needed)  
  \- Copy type (ad \= short, sales page \= long)  
  \- Platform (Twitter \= very short, landing page \= as long as needed)

  """,

  'voice\_decision': """  
  Copy must sound like a HUMAN wrote it — not AI, not a template.

  Rules for human-sounding copy:  
  \- Write like you talk. Read it out loud — does it sound natural?  
  \- Use contractions (you're, don't, it's — not "you are", "do not")  
  \- Use "you" more than "we" (reader-focused, not company-focused)  
  \- Short sentences. Then a long one for rhythm. Then short again.  
  \- One idea per paragraph. White space is your friend.

  \- Use specific numbers and details (not "many customers" but "2,847 customers")  
  \- Avoid buzzwords and jargon unless the audience uses them

CONVERSION\_PSYCHOLOGY \= """  
These are PRINCIPLES the engine uses when crafting copy.  
Not formulas — psychological understanding.

SPECIFICITY SELLS:  
  "Increase your revenue" is weak.  
  "Increase your revenue by 37% in 90 days" is strong.  
  Specific numbers, timeframes, and outcomes create believability.

LOSS AVERSION \> GAIN PROMISE:  
  People are more motivated to AVOID pain than to GAIN pleasure.  
  "Don't lose $5,000 in wasted ad spend" hits harder than "Save $5,000 on ads."  
  Use both — but lead with loss when audience is problem-aware.

SOCIAL PROOF SHORTCUTS DECISIONS:  
  "10,000 marketers already use this" reduces the decision burden.  
  People follow what others like them have done. Especially when uncertain.

RECIPROCITY CREATES TRUST:  
  Give value BEFORE asking for the sale.  
  Free guides, insights, tips — build goodwill that makes selling easier.

SCARCITY CREATES URGENCY:  
  Real scarcity (limited spots, limited time) accelerates decisions.  
  Fake scarcity destroys trust. Only use when genuine.

FUTURE PACING:  
  Help the reader IMAGINE life after using the product.  
  "Imagine opening your laptop on Monday morning to find 47 new leads waiting..."  
  Vivid future-state descriptions activate desire.

OBJECTION HANDLING:  
  Every reader has silent objections: "Is this worth the money?" "Will it work for me?"  
  "What if it doesn't work?" "I've tried things before and failed."  
  Great copy ADDRESSES these objections before the reader even thinks them.

ONE READER, NOT A CROWD:  
  Write to ONE person, not "customers" or "users" or "people."  
  "You" is the most powerful word in copywriting.  
  The reader should feel like this was written specifically for them.

MECHANISM \> PROMISE (HIGH SOPHISTICATION MARKETS):  
  In saturated markets, "lose weight fast" is invisible.  
  "Lose weight by fixing your cortisol window, not your calories" is new.  
  The HOW is more persuasive than the WHAT when the market has heard the WHAT a thousand times.  
"""
